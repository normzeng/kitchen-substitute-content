Substitute for Eggs in Cookies

Eggs in cookies bind the dough and control spread. Here is what to use instead, with exact amounts.

In a cookie, the egg is binder more than leavener — which is why egg swaps behave well here. The flax egg is the default answer: 1 Tbsp ground flaxseed + 3 Tbsp water per egg, rested 10 minutes until gelled. All amounts below are per egg, US and metric.

The swaps

  • Eggs
    • Flax egg — per 1 egg — 1 Tbsp ground flaxseed + 3 Tbsp water; rest 10 min until gelled — 7 g ground flaxseed + 45 ml water
      What changes in cookies: binds but doesn't leaven; the cookie bakes denser and chewier — many people prefer it
    • Water + oil + baking powder — per 1 egg — 1 1/2 Tbsp water + 1 1/2 Tbsp oil + 1 tsp baking powder — 22 ml water + 22 ml oil + 4 g baking powder
      What changes in cookies: slightly more tender, less rich — best in batters that already use oil
    • Unsweetened applesauce — per 1 egg — 1/4 cup unsweetened applesauce — 60 g applesauce
      What changes in cookies: moister, softer, cakey rather than crisp — good for soft-batch styles, wrong for thin crisp cookies
    • Aquafaba (chickpea liquid) — per 1 egg — 3 Tbsp aquafaba — 45 ml
      What changes in cookies: closest to egg behavior in light, whipped doughs; useless where eggs are pure binder

Cookie questions

Which swap keeps cookies crisp?
None of them fully — egg's moisture-to-structure ratio is part of what sets a crisp edge. The water + oil + baking powder version comes closest. For soft, chewy cookies the flax egg and applesauce are actually upgrades.
Can I just leave the egg out?
In some sturdy drop doughs, yes — the cookie spreads more and gets brittle. If the recipe has more than one egg, it needs the binder; use the flax egg.
