Substitute for Heavy Cream in Soup & Sauces

Cooking is the easy case for cream swaps — nothing needs to whip. Here are the exact amounts.

In soup and sauces, cream contributes fat and body, not structure, so rebuilding the fat is enough. Default answer: 3/4 cup milk + 1/4 cup melted butter per cup of cream. Stir the butter-milk in off the heat or at a bare simmer.

The swaps

  • Heavy cream
    • Milk + melted butter — per 1 cup heavy cream — 3/4 cup milk + 1/4 cup melted butter — 180 ml milk + 60 g melted butter
      What changes in soup: very close — keep the heat gentle so the butter doesn't split
    • Half-and-half + butter — per 1 cup heavy cream — 7/8 cup half-and-half + 1/8 cup melted butter — 210 ml half-and-half + 30 g butter
      What changes in soup: same fat math, lighter body
    • Coconut milk (regular, not light) — per 1 cup heavy cream — 1 cup regular coconut milk — 240 ml
      What changes in soup: coconut flavor carries — strong in pumpkin or tomato soup, welcome in curries

Soup questions

Will milk-based swaps curdle in tomato soup?
They can, if boiled hard in something very acidic. Lower-fat milks curdle faster; the butter in the swap helps. Finish the soup off the heat, add the swap warm, and don't re-boil it.
Can I use the milk + butter swap to whip?
No. Whipping needs cream's natural emulsion; rebuilt fat-in-milk will not hold air. For anything whipped, chilled coconut cream is the dairy-free route (1 cup, refrigerated overnight, then whipped).
