Vegan chocolate chip cookies

The classic recipe, changed in exactly three places.

A standard chocolate chip cookie is nearly vegan to begin with. Flour, both sugars, leavening, vanilla: already fine. The work is the butter, the eggs and the chips, and none of it is hard once the amounts are exact.

Unmarked lines carry over untouched. The marked lines are the whole conversion.

butter

1 cup

Stick-style plant butter, straight from the fridge, creamed for the full time the recipe asks. Sticks are built to butter's fat-to-water ratio, so they cream and bake the way the recipe expects. Tub spreads carry more water and the cookies turn cakey.

Watch the salt. Plant butters range from barely salted to aggressive; taste the dough before adding the recipe's salt.

eggs

2 Tbsp ground flaxseed + 6 Tbsp water

Two flax eggs. Stir the ground flaxseed into the water and give it ten minutes to gel. It ends up looking like egg white and behaving like it here: it binds. It doesn't leaven, but a cookie that creams butter and sugar for its lift barely notices.

The honest difference: a denser, chewier crumb. Plenty of people end up preferring it.

Two questions that come up

Could I just use oil for the butter?
For creamed cookies, no. If you want the oil route it's 3/4 cup oil per cup of melted butter, and it suits recipes that melt the butter anyway. Creaming needs a solid fat.
I've only got whole flaxseed.
Grind it finely first; whole seeds won't gel into a binder. Half a minute in a coffee or spice grinder does it.
