Substitute for Sour Cream in Baking

Sour cream tenderizes cakes and adds tang. The swaps are cultured cousins, measured exactly.

Full-fat Greek yogurt, one for one by volume, whisked smooth. Same cultured acidity, same tenderizing. Amounts below are per 1 cup of sour cream, US and metric.

The swaps

  • Sour cream
    • Greek yogurt (full fat) — per 1 cup sour cream — 1 cup — 240 g
      What changes in baking: slightly less fat, brighter tang — the standard baking swap, nearly invisible in chocolate and coffee cakes
    • Crème fraîche — per 1 cup sour cream — 1 cup — 240 g
      What changes in baking: richer, less tangy; the higher fat resists splitting under heat
    • Store-bought vegan sour cream — per 1 cup sour cream — 1 cup (cashew or coconut base) — 240 g
      What changes in baking: plant fats and cultures mimic the tang; flavor is brand-dependent

Baking questions

Can I use light sour cream or light yogurt?
It bakes, but you lose fat that was keeping the crumb tender, and light sour cream has stabilizers that can change texture. Full-fat swaps are the safe assumption behind these ratios.
Does the tang survive baking?
Partly. Heat mellows the cultured tang but keeps the tenderizing — the acidity has already done its job on the flour's gluten before the oven finishes it.
