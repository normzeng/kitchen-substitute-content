Gluten-free

Gluten-free vanilla cake

One ingredient decides this bake. Spend the care there.

A gluten-free cake holds or collapses on the flour, and the difference between those outcomes is xanthan gum. A 1-to-1 blend that contains it substitutes straight across, measured exactly like flour. A blend without it, in a recipe written for wheat, is how sad flat cakes happen.

Buttermilk and eggs stay, since gluten-free isn't dairy-free. Everything else is the classic cake.

all-purpose flour

2 cups

Two cups of the blend, leveled with a knife like flour. Don't pack it and don't round up; the blends are starchier than wheat and less forgiving of casual measuring.

Crumb: slightly denser and more fragile than wheat, browning a touch differently. Same flavor, same moisture.

About that flour

Can I use almond flour instead?
Not in this recipe. Almond flour doesn't build structure, and this cake leans on it. The 1-to-1 blend with gum is the swap that holds.
My cake cracked across the top.
Common with GF batters: the gum holds the rise without gluten's stretch, so the top splits. Cosmetic, not a failure.
