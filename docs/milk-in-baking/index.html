Substitute for Milk in Baking

Milk in a bake adds liquid, a little fat, and the protein that browns the crust. Here is what to pour instead, exactly.

Soy or oat, unsweetened, straight across: 1 cup for 1 cup. In cakes, muffins and quick breads the difference doesn't survive the oven. Where milk's protein matters — dinner rolls, enriched doughs — the water + butter build below is the better swap.

The swaps

  • Milk
    • Soy or oat milk — per 1 cup milk — 1 cup unsweetened soy or oat milk — 240 ml
      What changes in baking: slightly less protein for browning; flavor varies by brand — soy behaves closest
    • Water + butter — per 1 cup milk — 1 cup water + 1 1/2 tsp melted butter — 240 ml water + 7 g butter
      What changes in baking: butter restores the milkfat, water the liquid — less browning and richness overall
    • Evaporated milk, diluted — per 1 cup milk — 1/2 cup evaporated milk + 1/2 cup water — 120 ml evaporated milk + 120 ml water
      What changes in baking: a lightly cooked-milk flavor — evaporated milk is just concentrated milk

Baking questions

Does the swap change if the recipe scalds the milk?
Older enriched doughs scald milk to disable a whey protein. With soy or oat milk there is nothing to disable, so you can skip the scalding step and just warm it for the yeast.
What about almond milk?
It works at 1:1 too, but it is lower in fat and protein than soy, so browning and crumb richness drop a little further. Fine in pancakes, less ideal in enriched breads.
