Substitute for Buttermilk in Pancakes

The batter is half-made and the carton is empty. Here are the exact amounts, for dairy and dairy-free.

Buttermilk in a pancake batter has two jobs: feed the baking soda its acid, and tenderize. The swap does both. Stir 1 Tbsp lemon juice or vinegar into milk to reach 1 cup, give it ten minutes, and the batter behaves.

The swaps

  • Buttermilk
    • Milk + lemon juice or vinegar — per 1 cup buttermilk — 1 Tbsp lemon juice or white vinegar + enough milk to reach 1 cup; rest 10 min — 240 ml milk + 15 ml acid
      What changes in pancakes: slightly thinner batter — pancakes spread a touch wider and lacier
    • Plain yogurt, thinned — per 1 cup buttermilk — 3/4 cup plain yogurt + 1/4 cup milk, whisked smooth — 180 ml yogurt + 60 ml milk
      What changes in pancakes: slightly thicker result — extra-tall, fluffy stacks
    • Soy or almond milk + acid — per 1 cup buttermilk — 1 Tbsp lemon juice or vinegar + enough soy or almond milk to reach 1 cup; rest 10 min — 240 ml plant milk + 15 ml acid
      What changes in pancakes: faint plant-milk flavor — the dairy-free path, soda still gets its acid
    • Milk + cream of tartar — per 1 cup buttermilk — 1 3/4 tsp cream of tartar + enough milk to reach 1 cup — 8 g cream of tartar + 240 ml milk
      What changes in pancakes: nothing flavor-wise — a dry acid, same reaction

Pancake questions

Can I just use plain milk?
Only if you also drop the baking soda or your pancakes will be flat and soapy-tasting: soda with no acid leaves a metallic taste and no lift. If the recipe is all baking powder (no soda), plain milk works fine.
Does the rest time matter?
Yes — 10 minutes. The acid needs that time to curdle the milk. Mix your dry ingredients while it sits and the timing solves itself.
