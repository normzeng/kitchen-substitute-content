Substitute for Butter in Cookies

Butter is flavor, fat and structure in a cookie. Each swap below trades a different one — pick by what your cookie can afford to lose.

First question: is your recipe creaming butter with sugar, or melting it? Melted-butter recipes absorb swaps almost unchanged. Creamed recipes lose the air that creaming whips in, so expect spread and texture shifts. Default answer: stick-style plant butter, 1:1.

The swaps

  • Butter
    • Vegan butter / margarine — per 1 cup butter — 1 cup, stick-style plant butter — 227 g
      What changes in cookies: closest match — brand-dependent salt and browning, so taste the dough's salt
    • Coconut oil — per 1 cup butter — 1 cup coconut oil, solid (refined for neutral flavor) — 227 g
      What changes in cookies: faint coconut note if unrefined; keeps solid-fat structure for creaming
    • Olive or vegetable oil — per 1 cup butter (melted uses only) — 3/4 cup oil — 170 ml oil
      What changes in cookies: spreads more, bakes up moister and denser — no butter flavor
    • Applesauce (half swap) — per 1 cup butter — replace up to HALF the butter 1:1 with applesauce (1/2 cup butter = 1/2 cup applesauce) — 1:1 by volume for the replaced half
      What changes in cookies: softer, very moist, less rich — a soft-batch move, never for crisp cookies

Cookie questions

Why do oil cookies spread more?
Butter is about 80% fat and 15% water; the water turns to steam in the oven and the milk solids set. Oil is 100% fat with no structure to set, so the dough flows further before the flour sets.
Which swap for chocolate chip cookies specifically?
Stick-style plant butter if you have it, solid coconut oil if you don't. Save the oil version for recipes that already melt the butter.
