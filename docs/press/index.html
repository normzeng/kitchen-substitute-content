Story angles

1. The abandoned category

The main paid kitchen-substitutions app on the App Store was last updated in November 2019, rates 3.4★ from 29 ratings — and still charts in paid Food & Drink (verified 2026-09-26, US store). People pay for crashes mid-recipe because the moment it serves — you're out of buttermilk with a cake half-mixed — is frequent and desperate. Kitchen Substitute is the modern rebuild.

2. Content is the product

The content bar: US and metric amounts for all 363 substitutes ("1 cup milk + 1 tbsp lemon juice, rest 10 min" — not "add to taste"), a function note, trade-offs, and a warning when a swap breaks baking chemistry. 361 of 363 substitutes include a full ratio breakdown; the exceptions (curing salt) give safety guidance instead of numbers, on purpose. The same dataset powers free web pages for every ingredient.

3. Privacy as architecture

No analytics SDK exists in the binary. Updates arrive as signed content packs over one narrow channel — atomic swap, tamper/rollback rejected and tested — so the app improves without ever learning who you are.

Long: Kitchen Substitute (iOS, $0.99) gives home cooks ingredient substitutions with exact amounts — 155 ingredients and 363 substitutes, ranked by what's already in the pantry, each with a function note, trade-offs, and baking-safety flags. Diet filters cover vegan, gluten-free, dairy-free and nut-free needs. The app is fully offline with no ads, accounts, or analytics; content updates arrive as cryptographically signed packs.
